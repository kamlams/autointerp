## Explanations

**2565** (density 97.832%, det 0.625, fuzz 0.42500000000000004) — *early sequence position*: Fires on nearly every token as a dense positional/context signal: strongest at positions 1-3 and decaying with sequence position, and most often silent on the first token, especially content words. Function words and punctuation, particularly after sentence boundaries, boost activation, while rare subwords and repeated characters at late positions are the main non-firing exceptions. It does not track topic, language, or specific word meaning.

**18871** (density 28.821%, det 0.575, fuzz 0.6) — *function words and sequence starts*: This feature fires on closed-class function words and punctuation at rates well above baseline, with activation strongly boosted at the start of a sequence so even content words at position 0 fire strongly. It fires most strongly on the first few tokens and on function words following other function words (e.g., out of, you can, will be). It does not fire on mid-sequence content words or subword tokens, and a function word like "have" can fail to fire when it follows a content word.

**8307** (density 25.073%, det 0.8, fuzz 0.825) — *function word and punctuation detector*: It fires on common English function words and punctuation, especially determiners like “the” and “a”, and stays silent on content words, digits, and most verbs/nouns. Firing is strongest when “the” or “a” starts a noun phrase after a preposition, conjunction, comma, or sequence start, and early positions produce stronger activations than later ones.

**8333** (density 24.672%, det 0.675, fuzz 0.4722222222222222) — *the and human nouns*: This feature fires on every occurrence of "the" and, more strongly, on human-related nouns/pronouns such as people, they, who, children, school, and students, especially in narrative prose and at the start of sequences. It also fires weakly on many surrounding function words in such passages, but stays silent on abstract nouns, acronyms, abbreviations, parentheses, and technical vocabulary, where even "the" fires only weakly.

**856** (density 24.651%, det 0.575, fuzz 0.525) — *dense 'the' and phrase-initial*: This dense feature fires on ~25% of all tokens, but its most reliable trigger is the token "the" (97% of occurrences), with other common function words, punctuation, and newlines firing at 30-40% and thus above the 24.7% baseline. It fires most strongly on "the" and on the first few tokens of a sequence or after prepositions/punctuation (i.e., at phrase/sentence starts), while weak firing is widespread later in text. It tends to stay silent on subword fragments, digits, spaces, and math symbols, though these can fire weakly at sequence starts.

**25327** (density 14.395%, det 0.875, fuzz 0.55) — *third-person narrative pronouns and past-tense verbs*: This dense feature fires throughout narrative prose about people, most reliably on third-person pronouns (he/she/him/her/his, 61-74% of occurrences) and on past-tense forms like "was"/"had" (49-51%), especially right after a pronoun. It fires strongly at the start of a sequence (mean activation 2.1 vs ~0.8 later) and on those pronouns/verbs, while later firings are weak spillover on common words and punctuation. It stays silent in code, technical reports, and other non-narrative registers.

**27007** (density 14.259%, det 0.775, fuzz 0.825) — *to-phrase function words*: This feature fires on common grammatical/function words and punctuation, with a strong preference for "to" and the word immediately after it, especially at the start of a sequence. It fires most strongly on sentence-initial "to" phrases like "to apologize" or "to focus on the", and on "to" after verbs like get/talk/up; later-position firings on other function words are weak and diffuse. It does not fire on content words such as "Experiment", "tempted", "memory", "well", or "tele".

**6229** (density 13.335%, det 0.6499999999999999, fuzz 0.7) — *comma clause-initial function words*: This feature fires on commas and on the function-word string that opens a clause/phrase right after a comma or coordinating conjunction, with the strongest activations when a sequence starts with a comma and the burst continues over the next few words. It fires weakly on other punctuation and on common function words elsewhere, but not on the content nouns/objects that complete the phrase or on function words not at a comma/conjunction boundary.

**17751** (density 12.353%, det 0.575, fuzz 0.75) — *definite article 'the'*: The feature fires on 100% of occurrences of the token " the" (lowercase with leading space), which is the only reliable trigger; it also fires weakly on other common function words and punctuation (to, and, ., a, of, comma) at 15-28% rates, and on a few positive adjectives (good, well, great, better, useful, favorite, amazing) among its strongest activations. Strong firing is overwhelmingly caused by " the" (96% of the top 2,000 firings), but sequence-initial content words, especially positive/evaluative ones, can also produce very strong activations (e.g., the maximum on "great" at position 0). It does not fire on all common words (contrasts show "was", "roof", "building", "generous" silent), so the non-"the" firings are a noisy long tail rather than a separate clean pattern.

**30772** (density 9.732%, det 0.675, fuzz 0.65) — *start-of-segment capitalization*: The feature fires on text-segment boundaries: the first token of a sequence, newlines, periods, commas, and the capitalized/uppercase tokens that follow them, with the strongest activations at position 0 and on single capital letters like " C" or " A". Activation decays over the next few tokens, producing weak firings on common words near boundaries. It does not fire reliably on mid-sentence lowercase words, and its apparent firing on frequent words like "the" and "a" is weak and frequency-driven rather than a specific trigger.

**8942** (density 8.090%, det 0.85, fuzz 0.8) — *early-sequence punctuation detector*: This feature fires on punctuation marks—especially periods, commas, question marks, and closing quotes—and, more weakly, on common function words like "to", "and", "of", and "that", almost always within the first 15 tokens of a sequence. **17901** (density 7.732%, det 0.725, fuzz 0.7) — *indefinite article noun phrase*: This feature fires on the indefinite article "a"/"an" and the noun phrase it introduces, with the strongest activations when the article is the first token of a sequence (e.g., "a preview of", "an upcoming game"). **32469** (density 6.150%, det 0.775, fuzz 0.4473684210526315) — *Religious/spiritual discourse*: Fires in passages about God, Jesus, Christ, the Bible, salvation, and sin, with strongest activations on explicit divine names and religious keywords; it also fires weakly on surrounding function words in those passages and at sequence starts because many texts open with religious invocations. **4935** (density 5.636%, det 0.6, fuzz 0.675) — *text segment onset*: Fires at the start of a new text segment—after [START], after a newline, or after an introductory phrase—on the first few tokens: discourse markers, determiners/pronouns, auxiliary verbs, and boundary punctuation. Strongest at the very first token of a sequence or immediately after a newline, with strength decaying quickly with distance; weak firings are spillover on common function words mid-sentence, and it does not fire on the sentence following a period (only 2% of period-following tokens fire).

**4501** (density 4.361%, det 0.7, fuzz 0.625) — *past-tense narrative verb phrases*: This feature activates on tokens in past-tense clauses, peaking on past-tense verbs/auxiliaries and "didn't" contractions, and spreading to the function words of the verb phrase (to, a, in, by, the). **27002** (density 4.313%, det 0.625, fuzz 0.575) — *Recipe and cooking instructions*: Fires on tokens embedded in recipe/cooking passages, especially ingredient nouns, measurements, and imperative cooking verbs such as Add, cook, and serve; it fires most strongly on these central cooking words and at sequence starts, and weakly on surrounding function words in the same passages. **3127** (density 4.192%, det 0.775, fuzz 0.4) — *first-person narrative voice*: This feature fires on the function-word skeleton of first-person/experiential writing—pronouns (I, we, my, our, you), copulas/auxiliaries (was, had, is, are, 'm, 've, 'll), and nearby determiners/prepositions (a, to) and punctuation. **5048** (density 3.487%, det 0.775, fuzz 0.7749999999999999) — *after have/has/had/been*: It fires on the token immediately following “have”, “has”, “had”, or “been” (near-deterministically), and on “been” itself, marking the start of the verb phrase or complement after the auxiliary; it often spills over onto the next few words of that phrase with decaying strength. It fires most strongly when the auxiliary is at the start of a sequence (position 0–3), especially sentence-initial “have”/“has”, and weakly mid-sequence or on common function words. **29165** (density 3.384%, det 0.55, fuzz 0.525) — *space-in token detector*: The feature fires reliably and strongly on the token `" in"` (space + "in"), which accounts for nearly all high-activation firings; it also fires weakly on many common function words as spillover. **25450** (density 3.247%, det 0.725, fuzz 0.75) — *compound/collocation continuation detector*: The feature fires on the token immediately following a set of common content words (e.g., sand, sweet, paint, rub, salt, jewelry, fruit, food, love, art, name, thing, help) that serve as the first element of a compound, derived word, or common collocation. It fires strongly when that next token is a hyphen or a bound suffix (-s, -ing, -ed, -less) or a tightly fused compound element (ball, time, based, egg), and weakly when it is a function word or punctuation (of, in, for, and, comma, period). It does not fire on the trigger words themselves or on tokens not preceded by such a trigger.

**7535** (density 3.100%, det 0.525, fuzz 0.55) — *syntactic boundary onset*: Fires at clause/phrase boundaries and on the first token(s) of the next constituent, with the strongest activation at sequence start and weaker activation at later sentence/clause edges. **656** (density 2.756%, det 0.85, fuzz 0.975) — *post-indefinite-article token*: This feature fires on the token immediately after an indefinite article (a/an/A/An/another) with near 100% reliability, and also on the article itself when it starts a sequence. It fires strongly at sequence starts and for common content words like "lot" or "different", weakly for less predictable continuations, and does not fire in the absence of a preceding indefinite article except for occasional spillover onto "of" in quantifier phrases and a few following function words.

**32702** (density 2.144%, det 0.675, fuzz 0.8999999999999999) — *early-sequence article detector*: Fires on the articles "a", "an", "the" (and capitalized variants) when they occur in the first ~15 tokens of a sequence, most strongly when the article is the first token after [START]. **12379** (density 1.884%, det 0.625, fuzz 0.725) — *sequence-start function words*: This feature fires strongly on tokens near the beginning of a sequence, especially the first few positions, and more weakly on clause-initial function words and commas later on. **6584** (density 1.766%, det 0.95, fuzz 0.975) — *“to” token detector*: This feature fires on the word “to” in all its common token forms (`" to"`, `to`, `To`, `TO`, plus `à` and `unto`), independent of surrounding context. **6758** (density 1.766%, det 0.925, fuzz 1.0) — *"to" token detector*: This feature fires on essentially every occurrence of the word "to" (including "To", "TO", and the French "à"), regardless of whether it is an infinitive marker or a preposition, and stays silent on almost all other tokens. **36** (density 1.729%, det 0.9, fuzz 0.95) — *The " to" token detector*: This feature fires on essentially every occurrence of the exact token " to" (lowercase with a leading space), which is why its firing rate matches that token's corpus frequency and why the strongest 2,000 firings are all " to". **28019** (density 1.704%, det 0.65, fuzz 0.65) — *reported speech frame*: This feature fires on the grammatical scaffolding of reported speech: the speech verb itself (especially "said" at sequence start) and the following function-word chain ("that", "he", "to", "the", "was", quotes) that introduces the quoted or indirect content. **30268** (density 1.669%, det 0.75, fuzz 0.725) — *verb-preposition collocation*: The feature fires on the token immediately following a set of common action verbs (live, play, act, work, run, pay, walk, eat, sit, etc.), most often a preposition/particle that completes a phrasal verb such as "live in", "play with", "work with", or "pay off". **16389** (density 1.391%, det 0.625, fuzz 0.55) — *give verb construction*: This feature fires on the verb "give" and its inflections, plus the nearby function words that complete its argument structure (articles, prepositions, pronouns, conjunctions), especially in "give someone something" and "give up/on/to" patterns. It does not fire on these function words in general, and weak firings on other transfer verbs like "put", "take", "offer", and "donate" appear to be spillover rather than the core pattern.

**32421** (density 0.376%, det 0.8, fuzz 0.825) — *Verb-to-object transition*: Fires on the token immediately following a verb, especially verbs of consumption/transaction like eat, buy, purchase, sell, save, produce, catch, download, receive, kill. **22641** (density 0.367%, det 0.7, fuzz 0.7749999999999999) — *Trigger-word successor*: It fires on the token immediately after a small set of trigger tokens—most reliably "book", "books", "film", "super", "stock", "movie", "British", "York", "car", and bullet asterisks—so the fired-on token's identity is irrelevant (often punctuation/function words).
